# Photography,  illustration and icons


## General photography guidelines

### General photography guidelines

Every photograph tells a story. A strong photo stands out on a crowded website presentation or social feed and inspires the viewer to learn more. VCH values are about caring, learning, and striving for better results to deliver exceptional care: our photos should reflect this. Inspirational photos of real people demonstrating their passion for our values will help to bring our organization to life on any medium.  VCH photographs should show moments that stay with viewers and inspire them to think, learn, or act. 

Successful print and digital communications rely on quality photography and we hope this guide will help you navigate creating your own photography or assist you in briefing and producing shoots with VCH photographers or other professional freelance photographers.

General Tips:

- VCH aims to use photography as a first choice for all imagery, as opposed to clip art or other illustrations.
- The photos should capture team members naturally working together and connecting with patients or people. 
- Use depth of field to focus on a precise element to show a human connection or element of a medical device. 

- In terms of quality, look to create images that are bright, look naturally lit, and are warm or neutral coloured. 

- Find natural colours that we can associate with the VCH colour palette. 

- Avoid images with unnatural colored tints, special effects, or distortion. 

- Remember to showcase diversity of our staff, medical staff and the population we serve. Avoid sterotypes and try to counter sterotypes when possible (showcase male nurses, female physicians etc.).

- AI-generated images are not recommended for clinical, instructional, or trust-critical patient content. They should be used only for conceptual or decorative purposes.

## Human portraits

### Human Portraits

Capture human portraits in a natural environment, in their community or in their workplace.

- Make sure the background isn't too busy. 
- Use depth of field.

## Action shots

### Action shots

Share unexpected angle or viewpoints to bring interest and intrique to the photo.

- Focus on the main subject but showcase an active environment to bring life to the story.

- Capture natural and authentic moments rather than staged or posed.

- Avoid having too many people in the shot to keep focus on the main subject.

## Nature

### Nature

Photography of nature helps to give a sense of calm to our communications and can be a great replacement when photography of people or programs are difficult. 

- Avoid busy scenes of traffic on roads or stormy waters. 
- Look for bright, colourful images that reflect the diverse landscapes of our coastal region.

## Facilities and buildings

### Facilities and buildings

Prioritize shots from outside the facility so that people can recongize the entrance when visiting.

- Point of view should be eye-level (same as the visitor). 

- Try to get a view of the whole building if possible with the main entrance. 
- Ariel shots can work for larger campus' with multiple entrances. 
- Capture photos by daylight with blue sky or at sunset with a purple glow.

- Avoid the presence of emergency vehicles.

## Illustration

### Illustration

We prioritize the use of photography to show real people engaging with our staff and services. Illustration may be used when photography is not possible or to add warmth to difficult topics.

- Utalize the VCH colour palette when creating illustrations. If using stock illustrations, dowload the illustrator files and change to VCH colours.

- Illustration style should be more realistic than cartoonish. 
- Remember to showcase various body shapes and diversity to reflect our population.
