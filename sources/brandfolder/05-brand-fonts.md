# Brand fonts

Proxima Nova is the preferred brand font for creating formal collateral, such as public health advertising campaigns and signage. This font should be used by designers and agency partners. 

Calibri is our general use font due to it's accessibilty for staff and medical staff. This font is prefered for internal documents such as presentations, memos and emails. 

Do not use the fonts Proxima Nova and Calibri together in the same layout or document. Use only one font.

If you are creating a document with large amounts of text for people with visual difficulties, you may use the serif font family Palatino Linotype for the body copy because it is designed to be very readable. Larger header fonts should remain either Calibri or Proxima Nova. Palatino Linotype should not be used for corporate communications.

## Proxima Nova

### Proxima Nova

Proxima Nova, designed by Mark Simonson, is a geometric yet modern typeface suitable for myriad uses. This is the typeface we use when creating formal collateral, such as posters and signage. Most VCH staff do not have this typeface on their computer. 

Available in:
Thin, Thin Italic, Light, Light Italic, Regular, Italic, Medium, Medium Italic, Semibold, Semibold Italic, Bold, Bold Italic, Extrabold, Extrabold Italic, Black, Black Italic

## Calibri

### Calibri

Calibri, designed by Luc De Groot, is a modern sans serif typeface accessible through Microsoft Suite. This is the typeface we use for 
casual internal documents, such as presentations and emails. 

Available in:
Light, Regular, Italic, Bold, Bold Italic

## BC Sans 

### BC Sans

As a complement to Proxima Nova, it is recommended that BC Sans Typeface be used for Indigenous languages.

BC Sans is an open-source "living" typeface developed for government to improve the readability and delivery of digital services. It was designed to support special characters and syllabics found in Indigenous Languages in B.C.

When using BC Sans for Indigenous content, the whole content block may be replaced by BC Sans (ideal for longer form documents, where Indigenous character will be used repeatedly). 

Single Indigenous words may us BC Sans only for less text intensive uses such as signage. When typing out an Indigenous language word, the entire word should be replaced by BC sans, not individual characters.
